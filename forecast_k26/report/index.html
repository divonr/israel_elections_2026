Knesset 26 Seat Forecast

A single seat forecast for every list and a single national turnout figure. Built from 82 published polls, eight past elections' polling errors, and ballot-box results back to 2003. Each correction is chosen by out-of-sample tests, not by taste.

Method in brief

Data and verification

  • True results: official Central Elections Committee ballot-box files for Knesset 16–25 (2003–2022): votes25…votes21.bechirot.gov.il and the CEC files on data.gov.il. Double-envelope rows (soldiers, diplomats, prisoners) are kept for national totals and excluded from geography. Party letters are mapped to lists and to nine party families. Computed seat allocation reproduces the official 2022 result exactly.
  • Historical polls: every seat projection in the final list-configuration table of the Wikipedia polling pages for 2009–2022 (the aggregator the competition recommends), parsed with a rowspan/colspan-aware grid. Firms are linked into lineages (Midgam, Kantar/Teleseker, Lazar/Panels, Direct Polls, Maagar Mochot …).
  • Second historical poll source: every seat poll of the final 30 days before each election 2009–2022 was collected again, independently, from Hebrew Wikipedia. Hebrew Wikipedia has no poll table for 2009, 2013 or 2015, so for those years the polls came from archived originals (the outlets' own articles, bhirot2009/2013 poll pages, IMRA digests, Project 61, Knesset Jeremy). A separate agent then re-checked every row of each file against the sources and fixed 22 rows; 3 missing polls were added (step 04b). Of the 299 polls in this source, 272 match an English-Wikipedia poll (same election, same firm, dates within 2 days, or 4 days for near-identical tables). 262 of those have identical seat tables, and the mean difference is 0.06 seats. The 27 polls missing from English Wikipedia were added to the history, 12 of them in the final fortnight that the back-test uses (2013: 8, 2015: 3, 2021: 1). All results below use this merged history.
  • 2026 polls: the Wikipedia 2026 page merged with the two AI agents' collections. All 48 polls found in both sources agree seat for seat (0 discrepancies). Seven agent-only polls were checked against their sources (e.g. the i24 23.10 article and the DRI report on the CEC site). CEC poll reports supply the percentages of lists polled below the threshold. Every poll's seats are converted to votes exactly as in the historical calibration: a list with s seats gets the midpoint of the vote range that Bader-Ofer maps to s seats, (s + ½)·T/(S + J/2). This reproduces the CEC raw percentages closely; for example Israel Hayom 22 Oct gives Hendel 3.42% vs 3.7% raw and Likud 16.35% vs 16.5%. The measured corrections and error sizes therefore apply unchanged. Using the reports' raw percentages instead is shown as a sensitivity variant.
  • Race facts: Blue & White withdrew on 19 Oct (CEC-confirmed 21 Oct) and is set to 0. Surplus-vote agreements (Yalkut HaPirsumim 15045): Together–Yisrael Beiteinu, Joint List–Ra'am, RZP–Zehut–Likud, UTJ–Shas, Democrats–Yashar. Electorate: 7,331,981 eligible voters.

Voter segments from the true results

About 100,000 ballot-box results (and, separately, ~12,000 settlement results) from 10 elections are pooled in one 9-family vote-share space, so a segment means the same thing in every election.

Four clustering algorithms were run, each at K = 4–14 (NMF up to 8, the number of families) and at two units (ballot box and settlement): k-means and a Gaussian mixture on Hellinger-transformed shares, Ward hierarchical clustering, and non-negative matrix factorisation (soft archetypes). Internal validity favours few segments: silhouette, Calinski–Harabasz and stability peak at K = 4. Internal indices cannot say which segmentation explains pollster error, though, so the deciding test is downstream (next sections).

The 2026 polls: one campaign, two pictures

In the final ten days, three firms (Channel 14's Filber/NEXT DATA, i24's Direct Polls and the independent DRI poll) put Likud near its 2022 level. The six mainstream firms show Likud losing about a quarter of its 2022 vote, mostly to Yashar! and Together. The Arab-list total is almost the same at every firm. The gap is about one question: will disaffected 2022 Likud voters come back? Filber's panel says 90% will; a Midgam panel in May said only 58% would "definitely" vote Likud again. The firms also treat undecided voters and 2022-vote weighting differently.

History does not settle it. Direct Polls leaned only 1.5–2.7 bloc seats to the right of the field in 2020–2022, and in 2022 it was the closest firm on the bloc. The 2026 gaps (+13 for Channel 14, +8 for i24) are three to five times its largest past lean. NEXT DATA is a new firm, co-owned by Channel 14's owner, with response rates of 1–6% and a formal petition over its filings. DRI's methodology is undocumented. The backtest found no weighting that beats one vote per firm, so each firm counts once, and the disagreement becomes an explicit uncertainty term. The call is robust: dropping Channel 14 or DRI, halving their weight, or giving the whole right-leaning camp one shared vote moves Likud by at most one seat (see the sensitivity table).

Is the disagreement real? Heterogeneity test across firms

Cochran's Q across the nine firms' means (binomial standard errors, effective sample size = total n / 1.5). I² is the share of between-firm variation that sampling error cannot explain. The disagreement is concentrated in Likud, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu, Shas and the two threshold lists, which is the axis the simulation's "which firms are right" shock follows.

Limitations and independent review

Before this version, the full pipeline went through an adversarial code review: four independent reviewers, each finding checked by a skeptical verifier who tried to refute it by re-running the code. Every issue that could move numbers was fixed and the whole chain re-run. The main ones:

  • lists that polls showed at 0 seats entered the bias estimates at placeholder shares; family errors are now size-weighted over measured lists only;
  • seats were converted to votes as seats/120, which put 4-seat lists below the threshold they had passed; the D'Hondt-midpoint conversion fixed a measurable under-confidence in threshold probabilities and improved the raw baseline from 22.5 to 20.0 seats per election;
  • NMF hard labels ignored archetype scale;
  • a 2014 poll was dated inside the 2015 final fortnight;
  • 58 duplicated 2015 ballot ids were double-counted at settlement level;
  • an undefined composition multiplier occurred when a party family was absent from the base election;
  • the segment model's baseline left out double-envelope votes (soldiers, diplomats and others, about 10% of votes). A perfect poll would still have been "corrected". An explicit double-envelope segment now makes an unbiased poll reproduce the official result exactly.

Known limitations that remain:

  • Only eight elections are available, so most method comparisons are not significant at 5%. That is why the final model averages over segmentations instead of choosing one.
  • Segmentations are fitted on all ten elections, including each held-out one. They use vote composition only, never poll errors. A verification re-fitted the NMF segmentations inside every backtest fold and got identical backtest scores.
  • The out-of-sample calibration covers the historical error terms. The 2026 firm-disagreement term is added deliberately on top, because the heterogeneity test shows it is real.

Reproducibility

Everything runs from the forecast_k26/code folder, in order: 01_download_data.py → 02_parse_results.py → 03_parse_wiki_polls.py → 04_build_poll_datasets.py → 04b_merge_hewiki_polls.py → 05_clustering.py → 06_poll_error_analysis.py → 06b_model_selection.py → 06c_final_factors.py → 06d_calibration.py → 06e_pollster_profiles.py → 07_forecast_2026.py → 07b_firm_heterogeneity.py → 08_turnout.py → 09_build_report.py. Shared mappings and the seat-allocation law live in config.py. Every intermediate table is in outputs/tables, and docs/DECISION_LOG.md logs each decision in order. README.md is the entry point.

Verified reproducible. reproduce_from_raw.sh rebuilds everything from the raw downloads in an isolated copy (steps 02–09). It reproduced the submitted seat vector exactly (zero difference for every list) and the same turnout, 71.2%. The full run takes about 17 minutes on a laptop CPU.
